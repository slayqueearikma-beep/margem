\chapter*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Executive Summary}

MABRID is conceived as a \emph{trusted} Moroccan digital marketplace: a multi-sided platform connecting buyers, local sellers, and professional service providers while embedding verification, community moderation, and enterprise-grade security into the operating model. This executive summary distils the full report for decision-makers, investors, and compliance stakeholders.

\section*{Strategic Positioning}

Morocco's digital economy is expanding through mobile adoption, e-commerce growth, and government programmes supporting entrepreneurship. However, fragmentation of local business discovery, limited trust signals, and uneven digital literacy create friction for both consumers and small enterprises. MABRID addresses this gap by combining curated business listings, reputation systems, city-based communities, and optional premium visibility for sellers---without positioning the platform as a payment intermediary in its initial phase.

\section*{Governance and Management}

The enterprise operates under a defined governance structure separating strategic oversight (board and executive leadership), operational management (product, trust \& safety, finance), and independent security \& compliance functions. Key performance indicators span marketplace liquidity (active buyers and sellers), trust metrics (verification rates, dispute resolution time), security health (incident mean time to respond), and unit economics (customer acquisition cost versus lifetime value).

\section*{Security Architecture}

Security is treated as a board-level priority. MABRID adopts a \gls{zt} strategy: identity-first access, least privilege, micro-segmentation, and continuous verification. Identity lifecycle management incorporates \gls{rbac}, \gls{mfa}, and conditional access policies. Data is classified, encrypted in transit and at rest, and backed up with tested recovery procedures. Threat management follows structured risk assessment, threat modelling, vulnerability management, and an incident response programme integrated with \gls{siem} and \gls{soar} capabilities on Azure (Microsoft Sentinel, Log Analytics).

\section*{Cloud and Infrastructure Strategy}

Microsoft Azure provides the primary cloud foundation, governed through Azure Policy, cost management, role-based administration, and infrastructure-as-code discipline. Disaster recovery objectives target defined recovery time and recovery point thresholds for critical marketplace services. Cost optimisation balances performance, resilience, and fiscal sustainability during national scale-up.

\section*{Legal and Ethical Compliance}

MABRID aligns with Moroccan Law 09-08 on personal data protection, consumer protection norms, and electronic commerce expectations, while applying \gls{gdpr} principles where processing affects European data subjects. Platform policies---terms of service, seller and buyer rules, content moderation, and dispute resolution---form an integrated governance layer. Ethical commitments include transparency, fair marketplace practices, accessibility, and responsible use of automated decision support.

\section*{Roadmap and Outlook}

A three-phase roadmap progresses from anchor cities to national coverage and selective international expansion. Financial sustainability relies on diversified revenue streams and disciplined cloud spending. Future capabilities include enhanced identity verification, business intelligence for sellers, and enterprise partnership programmes.

\section*{Recommendation}

Stakeholders should treat this report as pre-launch documentation suitable for investor due diligence, enterprise partnership discussions, and regulatory engagement with the \gls{cndp}. Formal legal review and independent security assessment remain prerequisites to public launch.
