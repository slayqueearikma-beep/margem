\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

\textbf{Background.} Morocco's accelerating digital transformation has created demand for trusted intermediation between consumers and local businesses. Multi-sided marketplaces can unlock economic participation, yet they introduce complex obligations in security governance, data protection, and platform accountability.

\textbf{Objective.} This report documents the strategic, organisational, and regulatory foundations of \textbf{MABRID}, a proposed trusted Moroccan digital marketplace. The analysis deliberately excludes implementation detail and instead concentrates on business vision, enterprise governance, security architecture, cloud strategy, legal compliance, and sustainable growth.

\textbf{Methodology.} The report synthesises established management frameworks (Business Model Canvas, Lean Startup, PMBOK-aligned planning), international security standards (\gls{iso27001}, \gls{nistcsf}, \gls{cis} Controls), cloud governance practices on Microsoft Azure, and Moroccan data protection law (Law 09-08) alongside \gls{gdpr} principles relevant to cross-border processing.

\textbf{Findings.} MABRID's competitive differentiation rests on verifiable trust, city-based community governance, transparent seller policies, and a Zero Trust security posture supported by continuous monitoring. A phased national expansion roadmap balances revenue diversification (subscriptions, verification services, enterprise partnerships) with cost discipline in cloud operations.

\textbf{Conclusions.} The report demonstrates that MABRID can be positioned as investment-ready documentation: a coherent business case, defensible security programme, and compliance-oriented operating model suitable for enterprise adoption and regulatory scrutiny in Morocco.
